> Start a car engine.
Inside, pistons move up and down about thirty times a second.
Every piston runs through the same four steps, called the four-stroke cycle.
> Now take in air.
The piston drops down and the intake valve opens.
It pulls in air and a fine mist of fuel.
> Squeeze it.
The valves close and the piston rises, squeezing the mix into a tenth of its space.
Squeezed air gets hot, and the mix is ready to burn.
> Light the spark.
A spark plug fires, a tiny lightning bolt that ignites the mix in a thousandth of a second.
The burning gas expands and shoves the piston down with huge force.
> Push out the exhaust.
The exhaust valve opens, and the piston rises again to push the burnt gas out.
> Repeat, four strokes at a time.
Each cylinder fires once every two turns of the crankshaft.
A four-cylinder engine fires about fifty times a second.
> Turn the wheels.
The up-and-down motion turns the crankshaft, and the crankshaft turns the wheels.
