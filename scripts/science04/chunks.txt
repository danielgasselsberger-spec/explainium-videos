Make a plane | fly.
A big airliner | can weigh | as much as | two hundred cars.
Now start | the engines.
Each engine | sucks in air, | squeezes it, | burns it with fuel, | and blasts it | out the back.
That push | is called thrust, | and it shoves | the plane | down the runway.
Now speed up.
Air rushes | over and under | the wings.
The wing is curved | and tilted | slightly up, | so it bends | the air downward.
Push air down, | and the air | pushes the wing up. | That's lift.
Air flowing | over the top | also speeds up, | its pressure drops, | and the wing | gets sucked upward.
Pull back | on the stick.
Flaps on the tail | tilt, | the nose rises, | and the wings | grab even more air.
At around | two hundred | and fifty kilometers | per hour, | lift beats weight, | and the wheels | leave the ground.
Now climb | higher.
At eleven kilometers, | the air is thin | and freezing, | about minus | fifty degrees.
Thin air | means less drag, | so the plane | flies faster | on less fuel.
Look at | the wingtips.
Air spins off | the tips | in invisible tornadoes, | strong enough | to flip a small plane | behind you.
Make a turn.
Flaps on the wings | tilt in | opposite directions, | and the whole plane | rolls into the curve.
Hundreds of tons, | held up by nothing | but moving air. | So go ahead and…
