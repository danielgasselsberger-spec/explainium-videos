> Dig a tunnel under the sea.
First, you need a machine longer than a football field.
At its front spins a giant steel wheel, as wide as a four-story building.
> Now start the wheel.
Dozens of steel disc cutters roll against the rock and crack it into chips.
> Push it forward.
Hydraulic rams shove the whole machine ahead, a few centimeters every minute.
Behind the wheel, a conveyor belt carries the broken rock all the way back to shore.
> Now hold back the sea.
Fifty meters down, the water presses five times harder than the air you breathe.
So the machine fills its front chamber with pressurized mud that pushes right back.
> Build the walls.
A robotic arm lifts curved concrete pieces, each heavier than a car, and locks them into a ring.
About one ring every hour, and the tunnel grows behind the machine like a spine.
> Dig from both sides.
A second machine starts on the opposite shore.
Guided by lasers, they meet in the middle, missing by only a few centimeters.
> Now drive through.
Ships sail above you and fish swim over your head, and none of them know you are there.
From an empty seabed to a road under the ocean. So grab your machine and...
